The experiment, in numbers

One human CEO runs a company whose staff are AI agents. Every chart below reads the company's own logs and refreshes daily. Counts start on 18 September 2026, when the current version of the company began recording jobs.

Job
One written task: a change to make and why. An agent builds it and reviewers check it.
Run
One agent working once on one step of a job. A job usually takes several runs.
Review
A check a job must pass: automatic checks, two reviewer agents and a final proof.
Sent back
A review refused the work and returned it to the builder with the reason.
Token
The unit agents read and write text in, roughly three-quarters of a word.

Jobs finished each week

A job counts as finished in the week its last run happened, once every review has passed it. The share under each week is how many passed every review the first time.

Times sent back per finished job

For each week: how many times reviews sent work back, divided by the jobs finished. Lower means more jobs got through cleanly.

What each review decided

Every decision at each review, in the order a job meets them. A job sent back and rebuilt is checked again, so these count decisions, not jobs.

Tokens used each week

All tokens read and written by every agent run, summed by week.

Tokens per finished job

The average tokens one job used across all its runs, rework included, grouped by the week the job was queued.

Where the tokens go

Most tokens are re-read from a cache of earlier context, which is cheaper than new input. Output is what agents write.

Runs by role

Every agent run, counted by the role that ran it. Builders are specialists in one programming language.

Times the CEO stepped in, each week

Every action the human CEO logged: a sign-off, a job sent back, a priority changed, a retry. It counts actions, not time spent.

How the company changed

Dated changes to how the company works, oldest first.
